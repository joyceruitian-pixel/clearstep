# ClearStep Video Presenter Notes

Target length: about 3 minutes 55 seconds. These are natural speaking prompts. Keep the words in your own voice and do not claim that the AI said something it did not say.

| Time | Slide | Speaking points and cues |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | 1. ClearStep | “Hi, this is ClearStep. I built it to make confusing homework directions easier to understand, so students can start with more confidence.” **[Advance to slide 2]** |
| 0:10–0:30 | 2. The problem | “Sometimes I understand the subject but not what the directions want. Students may not always have someone nearby to explain them, and that confusion can make it hard to begin. The ERIC source on this slide is the research source I used; I am not adding an unverified statistic.” **[Advance to slide 3]** |
| 0:30–0:40 | 3. The solution | “ClearStep changes complicated directions into a short explanation and clear steps. It explains the work without doing the work.” **[Advance to slide 4]** |
| 0:40–0:45 | 4. Live demo | “Now I’ll switch to the actual website and show how it works.” **[Switch to ClearStep website]** |
| 0:45–1:00 | Live website | Paste this prepared assignment: “Read pages 12–18 of your textbook. Write a one-paragraph response explaining the main character’s motivation. Include two pieces of evidence from the story and submit your response by Friday.” **[Paste demonstration assignment]** |
| 1:00–1:25 | Live website | Click **Explain Directions**. “I’m looking at the real response now.” Point to the simple meaning and numbered steps. **[Click Explain Directions] [Show generated steps]** |
| 1:25–1:42 | Live website | Check one step, then tap an underlined vocabulary word such as **motivation**. “This gives a short meaning without changing the original explanation.” |
| 1:42–1:55 | Live website | Show the grade-level control and, if available, the “What to turn in” area. “These controls help the student understand what to do next.” Skip optional views if the request is slow. |
| 1:55–2:00 | Live website | “The teacher’s original directions are still the main source.” **[Return to slideshow]** |
| 2:00–2:25 | 5. How AI works | “The student’s directions go to my backend. The backend sends them to OpenAI with ClearStep’s instructions, and the structured result comes back as a summary and steps. The API key is not in the browser.” **[Advance to slide 6]** |
| 2:25–2:50 | 6. Testing | “I tested different assignments, safety requests, saved progress, and Focus Mode. The final MVP Goal 4 rerun had 15 out of 15 fully right cases, and the Focus Mode tests all passed. Those are separate test groups, not one overall accuracy score.” **[Advance to slide 7]** |
| 2:50–3:15 | 7. Responsible AI | “ClearStep explains directions instead of completing homework, warns students not to share private information, and reminds them that the teacher’s directions come first. It also refuses harmful or off-topic requests.” **[Advance to slide 8]** |
| 3:15–3:45 | 8. Learning and next | “I learned that building an AI project is not only about making features. Testing, safety, and accessibility are just as important. Next I want more student feedback and more accessibility testing.” **[Advance to slide 9]** |
| 3:45–3:55 | 9. Thank you | “ClearStep is about helping students understand the task, not just finish it. Thank you.” **[End recording]** |

If a live request is slow or fails, do not pretend it is live. Use a real successful response recorded earlier and say that it was recorded earlier if you edit the clips together.
